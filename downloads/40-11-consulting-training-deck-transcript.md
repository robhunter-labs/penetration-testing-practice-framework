## Slide 2

Visible slide text:

- SERVICE BOUNDARY
- The desk has two connected jobs
- Open Practice Framework
- 02
- PARTNER SUPPORT
- Interpret a finding. Explain test evidence and general remediation direction. Clarify the retest question and route the next action.
- TEAM SUPPORT
- Peer-review technical evidence, scope and reports. Back up the on-duty tester on urgent or difficult work.
- Not a new-test intake, a policy-approval desk, or a shortcut around the issue record.

Presenter notes:

An on-duty tester owns the contact and continuity; peers help when the question demands more expertise. Do not imply a fully staffed team sits on consulting. New engagements and scope expansion follow their own intake.

## Slide 3

Visible slide text:

- INBOUND TRIAGE
- Classify before you answer
- Open Practice Framework
- 03
- 1
- What is being asked?
- 2
- Which finding / record?
- 3
- What evidence exists?
- 4
- Who owns the decision?
- 5
- What happens next?
- If it is a new test or a broader scope, route it to engagement intake—not the consulting queue.

Presenter notes:

Check the existing issue record before asking for information already provided. Distinguish a clarification, evidence question, retest/closure request, governance request, new engagement, and sensitive external request.

## Slide 4

Visible slide text:

- RESPONSE ANATOMY
- A useful reply has five parts
- Open Practice Framework
- 04
- ACKNOWLEDGE
- Show the question was received.
- GROUND
- Name the existing finding and exact evidence.
- ANSWER
- Give the technical explanation you can support.
- LIMIT
- Say what you cannot decide or what remains untested.
- NEXT
- Name the action, owner, and destination.

Presenter notes:

The reply is a service action, not a script. It should answer the actual question, preserve claim bounds, route policy and risk decisions to their owners, and leave a trace in the approved queue.

## Slide 5

Visible slide text:

- PEER SUPPORT
- The on-duty tester should not stand alone
- Open Practice Framework
- 05
- ANSWER
- Known method, complete evidence, clear scope, no new decision authority.
- PEER REVIEW
- Ambiguous reproduction, report quality, unfamiliar technology, conflicting evidence.
- ESCALATE
- Sensitive release, urgent safety/incident concern, policy or risk approval, cross-team ownership.
- Document the second opinion and the route used—not just the name of the person you asked.

Presenter notes:

Escalation is a trained and exercised support system. The on-duty tester remains accountable for acknowledgement and handoff, while the right authority makes the decision. Local roles and contacts must be filled in by the adopter.

## Slide 6

Visible slide text:

- PRACTICE CASE
- Scenario 1 · “I think this is a false positive”
- Open Practice Framework
- 06
- INBOUND
- An owner disputes a reported access-control issue. The original screenshot is ambiguous; the repro step may use the wrong account.
- BEST NEXT MOVE
- Acknowledge. Re-open protected evidence. Ask a peer to review role and request context. State what is and is not confirmed. Correct the record if needed.

Presenter notes:

Answer key: do not defend the severity reflexively, and do not accept a false-positive claim without checking. Peer review the identity and request evidence. If the finding is unsupported, correct it transparently.

## Slide 7

Visible slide text:

- PRACTICE CASE
- Scenario 2 · “Who fixes the shared component?”
- Open Practice Framework
- 07
- INBOUND
- A tested application and several other teams use the same component. The owner asks the desk to assign enterprise-wide remediation.
- BEST NEXT MOVE
- Explain the tested instance and evidence. Invite the relevant security and business partners to the owner discussion. Route broad-pattern ownership and scope through local governance.

Presenter notes:

Answer key: avoid saying the one test proved enterprise-wide exposure. The closing conversation can surface the broader question, but another owner and evidence path decide its footprint.

## Slide 8

Visible slide text:

- PRACTICE CASE
- Scenario 3 · “Send the report to this auditor”
- Open Practice Framework
- 08
- INBOUND
- A requester claims an urgent external audit need and asks for the full report and reproduction details by email.
- BEST NEXT MOVE
- Acknowledge without attaching. Verify requester, purpose, classification, and approval path. Route disclosure through the authorized owner; log exactly what is released.

Presenter notes:

Answer key: no silent external disclosure. Acknowledgement can precede verification; the first substantive artifact follows approval, redaction, and released-copy custody. This is not a reason to ignore the requester.

## Slide 9

Visible slide text:

- QUEUE CONTINUITY
- Handoff is part of the answer
- Open Practice Framework
- 09
- OPEN
- What came in? Which record and owner? What is the response clock locally?
- ACTIVE
- What was checked? Who reviewed it? What is pending from whom?
- CLOSED / ROUTED
- What answer went out? What next action and destination were named?
- A rotation is one staffed front door with team backup—not a string of private DMs.

Presenter notes:

Keep mailbox, queue, and retest/closure intake under shared visibility. Rotation staffing must flex for engagements and time off; a handoff identifies anything unacknowledged or at risk of being missed. The adopter chooses hours and response targets.

## Slide 10

Visible slide text:

- LOCAL DESIGN
- Adapt the desk to the load
- Open Practice Framework
- 10
- KEEP THE HUMAN BAR
- Technical judgment, peer challenge, sensitive disclosure, high-consequence escalation, and the final answer remain accountable.
- AUTOMATE WITH EVIDENCE
- Intake classification, duplicate detection, record lookup, draft assembly, SLA visibility, and handoff can assist—but require source checks, redaction, and human disposition.
- Measure whether questions are resolved well—not simply how many replies were sent.

Presenter notes:

This is a design space, not a claim that an AI agent or automation is deployed. A small team with low volume and strong vulnerability-management partners may use a rotation; larger demand can justify dedicated staffing. Local load and service quality determine the model.
