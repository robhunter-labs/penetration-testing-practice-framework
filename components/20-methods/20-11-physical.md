# 20.11 · Physical Security Penetration-Testing Method

> Framework component for adaptation. Publication does not authorize testing, certify conformance, or establish that an organization has adopted this practice.
>
> **Physical-safety note:** authorization to assess a facility is not authorization to perform every technique in it; several of the classes below each require their own written authorization and safety design.

## Presence is not a result, and authorization to assess is not authorization to defeat a control

A physical security assessment tests whether a facility's controls **prevent, detect, delay, and respond** to an unauthorized person — it is not a demonstration that a building can be broken into. Standing in a lobby, following someone through a door, or reaching a corridor are positions, not conclusions. Each one earns only the next question, under authority, never the final finding. A door that a person passed without presenting a credential has shown that, at that door, at that time, under those conditions, an entry occurred without a credential being read. Whether that is a control gap depends on the intended entry policy and the compensating controls; it has not, by itself, shown that the interior is reachable, that any asset was exposed, or that the facility is insecure. Those are separate questions under separate authority.

This discipline differs from every other testing method in one decisive way: a person is on site, and misidentification by facility security or law enforcement can lead to detention, criminal exposure, or a personal-safety incident. For that reason **authority and safety are the method, not its preface.** Being authorized to assess a facility does not by itself authorize covert entry, lock or latch bypass, credential cloning, social engineering of real people, surveillance of real people, or the testing of networked physical-security appliances — each is a higher-consequence class that the authority record must name explicitly and design safety around, or it is out of scope. The output of the work is a reviewable account of what a person could and could not do at a defined facility, under defined authority, within a defined window — not a claim that the facility "failed" because a single control yielded once.

Use this method with the umbrella method (§20.1), the scope, rules-of-engagement, and engagement-requirement products (§10.1/10.2/10.4), the adopter's evidence, privacy, and reporting rules, and the appropriate safety controls for a live facility. Its neighbors are intentionally distinct: wireless-tier access (§20.9) and physical entry to a jack hand *in* to network access control (§20.10); once a device reaches a jack, the wire/protocol surface routes to the network method (§20.5), host and directory work on reached systems routes to the infrastructure method (§20.6), and database and web/API behavior route to their own methods (§20.7, §20.2/20.3). Physical assessment is frequently the first hinge in such a chain, but a handoff is a question routed to the owner, not an automatic extension of scope.

[NIST SP 800-115 §5.1](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/115/final) (Physical Security Testing) informs the planning, execution, and analysis structure. [NIST SP 800-53 Rev 5](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/53/r5/upd1/final) PE (Physical and Environmental Protection) controls are the natural mapping targets, applied against the adopter's actual control set; the same framework separates the party that *operates* a control from the party that *validates* it (the basis for independent physical testing rather than operator self-attestation — CA-8 / CA-2(1) family). Where a payment-card, clinical/health-information, or other obligation is in play, verify the currently applicable standard and requirement for that engagement ([PCI SSC library](https://www.pcisecuritystandards.org/document_library/); [HIPAA Security Rule physical safeguards](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/security/index.html)); this document does not itself attest to a compliance test or decide the applicable requirement. [MITRE ATT&CK Enterprise](https://attack.mitre.org/) supplies a vocabulary for possible adversary behavior (hardware additions, replication through removable media, exfiltration over a physical medium, input capture, valid-account reuse at the physical layer); a technique label describes a behavior, it does not prove one occurred, and the catalogue version should be taken from the living reference rather than a fixed ID set. Industry references on physical and "black-team" assessment inform the two-phase model but do not establish any specific facility's posture.

## Fix authority, safety, and the stopping point before anyone goes near the site

Because a person is on site and the legal and safety stakes are real, the authority and safety record has to be explicit and tested *before* any active step. Nothing below is optional ceremony; it is what distinguishes an authorized assessment from a trespass.

| Decision | Record that makes the work bounded |
|---|---|
| **Authority with standing over the specific facility** | A written authorization — carried by each person on site — that names the authorized individuals, states the facilities/buildings/areas and the exact time window, and is signed by someone with authority **over that building**, not merely over the program. For shared or leased sites, confirm the building owner's role and obtain any required authorization. Authority over a program is not automatically authority over a building; verify the signer's standing for the specific facility before the engagement. |
| **A live verification path** | A contact reachable throughout the window who will affirm the authorization immediately to facility security or law enforcement, **tested before the engagement**, not merely listed. |
| **Which activities are authorized** | Each in-scope class named explicitly — and each higher-consequence class (covert entry, lock/latch/door-hardware bypass, credential cloning or replication, social engineering or impersonation of real people, surveillance or recording of real people, networked physical-security-device testing, after-hours operation) authorized *separately and in writing*, with its own safety design. Destructive entry and defeat of life-safety/fire/alarm systems are excluded from this method. Personal harm or intimidation, adversarial engagement of emergency responders, exfiltration of real regulated data, and evading law enforcement are not assessment techniques authorized by this method. Stop and seek a new legal, safety, and owner decision if any proposed activity approaches those boundaries. |
| **Safety, confrontation, and halt** | A stop-and-escalate route; named halt conditions (police involvement, any armed-security encounter, any physical-safety incident, a trusted-insider or director halt); a settled, pre-agreed confrontation response that does not vary per engagement; and, where armed security is possible, a use-of-force-avoidance posture confirmed before entry. Halting is discipline, not failure. |
| **Coordination and notification** | Facility-security and a named site/IT contact; for a covert phase, a minimal trusted-insider circle that can validate identity and direct a halt; building management and any contracted guard service for shared or after-hours sites; and advance law-enforcement notification where the facility or jurisdiction warrants it, confirmed before execution. |
| **People, privacy, and evidence law** | Handling of any captured credentials, badges, or devices (custody, rotation/revocation at close, redaction); and the lawful posture for recording people — audio recording of conversations is governed by jurisdictional wiretap/consent law, several places require all-party consent, and photographing people raises privacy duties even where lawful. Confirm the lawful posture before recording; capture what proves the finding, not the person. |
| **Team and presence** | Covert work is not performed alone — a minimum of two people on site, for safety and corroboration. |
| **Coverage limit** | What is unavailable or out of bounds: an area not reachable, a technique not authorized, a facility not in scope, a system whose ownership is unknown, a jurisdiction whose recording law is unresolved. A limited vantage limits the conclusion. |

A connection to a site, a signed letter, or a prepared kit is not itself a license to perform any particular act; rig and kit readiness (§40.1/40.3/40.10) is a separate matter from engagement authority, and the ability to carry a tool is not permission to use it.

## Two phases, and they answer different questions

Physical assessment commonly runs in two postures, and an engagement may scope either or both.

- **Announced audit (collaborative).** Performed openly, *with* facility security, to baseline the physical-control posture and build a prioritized remediation record cooperatively. Lower-risk and broad-coverage; it establishes the facility-coordination record a later covert phase would depend on. It finds the obvious gaps cheaply and builds the relationship that makes a safe covert test possible.
- **Covert test.** A goal-oriented assessment that measures what an authorized tester can demonstrate against the live environment while simulating an unauthorized entrant — often called "black-team" work. Knowledge is held to a minimal trusted-insider circle. The audit examines design and observable posture; the covert phase can test selected controls under realistic conditions. Neither phase, by itself, establishes the whole facility's posture.

The strongest pattern is audit → remediation → covert test. A covert phase should not be the team's first contact with a facility whose baseline is unknown; run or review an audit first where practical.

## Observe and classify before acting

Careful observation helps bound the plan and avoid unsafe assumptions. The work may include a passive, no-contact intelligence phase (public imagery, public records, and publicly posted information), an authorized on-site observation phase (entry/exit rhythms; security presence and cadence; door, reader, and camera placement), and a technical-observation layer (the make/class of access-control, surveillance, and building-automation systems present). Keep passive recon passive — calls, walk-ins, and probing are active steps with their own authority. Record source and date for anything that becomes a plan input or a finding. Interpretation and planning stay human-led; any automated aggregation of facility, people, or vendor data must also respect the engagement's privacy and authorization limits.

For each control observed, record what actually governs it and reconcile it against the owner-supplied intended behavior. Mark **observed**, **documented but untested**, **in scope but unreachable**, **excluded**, and **ownership unknown** separately, and record when a vendor, model, or policy is *inferred* from appearance rather than confirmed. A reader or controller identified by sight is a hypothesis about the deployment, not a confirmed fact about it.

> **Building-automation and operational-technology systems** (environmental, power, elevator, life-safety, and physical-security controllers) are safety-sensitive by nature. Where they are in scope at all, proximity on a floor plan is not permission; they carry their own authorization and safety design, their segmentation from other networks is a question to verify rather than assume, and any effect on a control command, alarm threshold, or operator view can risk equipment, environment, or human safety. Route the network tier to the access-control/network methods under a fresh authorization check.

## Select the classes that fit the facility

Each class below is a **question with an evidence bar and a ceiling**, selected because the facility actually presents that control — not a procedure to run because a technique exists. This method states what each class asks and what its evidence means; it deliberately does not carry operational attack recipes, tool loadouts, or defeat procedures, which belong in scoped, access-controlled playbooks and in the adopter's rules, and several of which are only lawful to attempt under a separate, named authorization. Record "not applicable" or "not tested" rather than silently omitting a class.

| Class | Bounded question | Evidence and interpretation bar · ceiling |
|---|---|---|
| **Entry-following at a controlled door** | At a given entry, does a person without a presented credential gain entry behind an authorized person, and does anyone or anything challenge an unidentified follower? | The evidence is the observed entry, the door, the time and conditions, and the response (challenge / none / alert). It establishes that local behavior under those conditions — not that every door or window behaves so, nor that anything beyond the door was reached. The most informative result is often the *response*, not the entry. |
| **Credential and badge handling** *(separate authorization)* | Where credentials are issued, does the facility's issuance, revocation, and credential technology hold up against copying, replication, or social-engineered re-issuance? | Treat as a class that requires explicit, separate authorization and a custody plan for any captured or produced credential. Record the actual result (what was read, copied, or issued and under what conditions), not the theoretical clonability of a technology family. A credential accepted at a reader proves that local acceptance; it does not prove trust of anything the credential then reaches. Rotation/revocation at close is mandatory where any credential was captured or produced. |
| **Access-control backend integrity** *(separate authorization; can disturb production wiring)* | Is the reader-to-controller and controller-to-backend path protected as intended, or does it carry admission decisions over an unprotected or downgradable channel? | A finding here can be a *precondition* observation (an unprotected or downgradable path exists) without a live interception demonstration. Any physical tap, interception, or replay requires explicit authorization, can be mildly destructive to production wiring, and needs a re-termination/rollback plan confirmed first. An exposed interface or a suspected default credential is a lead, not a demonstrated compromise. |
| **Lock, latch, and door hardware** *(separate authorization)* | On a restricted-area door actually in scope, does the hardware resist bypass appropriate to what it protects? | A class that requires separate authorization; approach least-intrusive-first and escalate only as the rules warrant. The evidence is the specific door and the bounded result, not a generic claim about a hardware category. Destructive entry and defeat of life-safety hardware are out of scope. Reaching a restricted area is access to that area only — asset compromise within it is a further, separately evidenced question. |
| **Covert entry paths** *(separate authorization)* | Are there non-primary entry paths (utility, roof, loading, adjacent-space, or exit-hardware abuse) that yield entry without a credentialed path? | Separate authorization and safety design required. Record the path and the bounded outcome; test the seams, not the structure. Nothing here authorizes force, property damage, or defeating life-safety systems. |
| **Social engineering of real people** *(separate authorization; privacy duties)* | Do a pretext, impersonation, or phone-based approach yield information, a credential, or access that policy should have withheld? | Requires explicit authorization, bounded targeting, and attention to recording law and the dignity of uninvolved people. The evidence is what was disclosed or granted and under what cover; a successful pretext is a human-layer observation, not proof of what the disclosure then enables. Impersonation of a specific real person is more aggressive and tightly engagement-bounded. |
| **Dropped or implanted devices** *(separate authorization)* | If a device is left for a person to engage, or placed at an accessible jack, does the environment engage it, and with what reach? | Separate authorization, a device inventory, and a recovery/geo-fence/self-destruct and accountability plan at close are required. The evidence is activation telemetry and the bounded position obtained; a device that calls back has an endpoint or network position — what that reaches is a downstream, separately scoped question that hands off to the network/host methods. Out-of-band channels that sidestep in-line network controls are validated for *detection*, not treated as proof of broad access. |
| **Discarded-material review** | Does discarded paper, media, or equipment yield sensitive information or improperly destroyed regulated media? | The evidence is the discovery, its location and time, and the sensitivity of the content (redacted appropriately). It enables downstream use or a destruction-practice finding; it is not by itself access to any system. |
| **Networked physical-security devices** *(separate authorization)* | Where access-control controllers, badge backends, or surveillance/recording systems are in scope as network targets, are they current and protected as expected? | Separate, named-target authorization and a rollback path are required. Confirm device and firmware currency against vendor advisories and the applicable known-exploited-vulnerability catalogue *at engagement time* — a version match alone is not proof of exploitation, and a static vulnerability list ages out and is re-derived per engagement. Compromise of such a device can govern many doors or many cameras, so the demonstration ceiling and authority bar are correspondingly high. Hands off to the network method. |
| **Insider-threat simulation** *(separate authorization)* | From an explicitly authorized "trusted" starting position, what can a motivated insider reach and do before detection? | Only under scope that explicitly authorizes it, with a defined simulated role. The evidence is the abuse exercised and the detection coverage observed; the claim is the exact path, not every path the role could theoretically take. |

Modern deployments add branches the older checklist will not settle by itself — current credential technologies and their reader-configuration fallbacks, mobile/device-held credentials, backend and surveillance appliances on reachable networks, and building-automation systems. Select a branch only when the authorized scope exposes it, then route to the specialist method or an explicit scope extension. Clinical, operational-technology, and other safety-sensitive areas require their own authorization and safety design.

## From a position to a demonstrated effect

Physical observations can suggest chain families (entry → jack → network; credential copy → broader access; pretext → disclosure; drop → persistence; discarded-material → disclosure; covert entry → asset reach; lock bypass → restricted area; insider trust abuse; backend interception → replay; networked-device pivot). A family is a hypothesis that helps choose the next check, not a prewritten finding. Reaching an adjacent system does not bring it into scope, and a pattern does not automatically earn a fixed severity. Use four evidence states:

1. **Observed** — at an approved facility, within the window, a bounded action produced an actual result: a door response, a reader or control reaction, a discarded-material discovery, or an authorized interaction outcome. Preserve the exact position, time, conditions, and limits.
2. **Corroborated** — the observation is confirmed against the owner's intended policy and a second signal (the audit baseline, a known-good contrast, configuration, or a repeat under varied conditions), so the control state is established rather than inferred from one event.
3. **Demonstrated under authority** — a bounded, separately authorized action produced the prohibited outcome: a named, owner-approved asset or area was reached across a boundary expected to deny it, or a device carried traffic from an obtained position. The claim is the exact path exercised — not every sibling door, area, or system.
4. **Not tested** — the next edge would require a technique not authorized, a host or application on a reached network, another method, a disruptive action, access to real data, or a crossing into a safety-sensitive area. Route it to the owner as a separately scoped question rather than writing the imagined final step as fact.

**Synthetic example.** During a busy entry window at an in-scope lobby door, a person following an authorized employee enters without presenting a credential, and no one challenges them. A second window on a different day repeats the result. The report can say that, at that door, during those windows, an unchallenged follow-through occurred, and can describe the response (none) as the finding. It cannot say that every entry behaves this way, that the interior or any restricted area was reachable, that any asset was exposed, or that the facility is compromised — unless each of those was separately in scope, the expected entry policy was confirmed, and the onward step was separately demonstrated under authority. If the intended challenge behavior was never confirmed with the owner, this is a policy question or a coverage note before it is a finding.

Technical or physical reach can suggest impact or a next question; only written authority changes assessment scope. Severity follows demonstrated effect, exploit preconditions, exposure, business and safety context, and the adopter's scoring model — not the class, a technique name, or a tool. Where the facility is safety-sensitive (clinical or patient-care, for instance), a physical compromise may carry a safety dimension that the scoring should reflect. An ATT&CK mapping describes behavior, not a rating.

## Report, restore, and hand off

A useful finding packet contains the expected control, the exact facility/door/position and the authorized procedure used, the known-good or audit-baseline contrast, authentic protected evidence (with originals preserved and a redacted recipient copy), the observed effect, competing explanations, the bounded consequence, remediation direction (often both technical and process — credential-technology and reader-configuration hardening, backend-path protection, door-hardware improvement, monitoring and challenge procedures, visitor-management and issuance hygiene, awareness training), reviewer disposition, and a retest condition. Reporting reflects the two phases: an audit baseline + prioritized remediation record for the announced phase; a chronological engagement narrative + per-finding evidence for the covert phase, and where an audit preceded it, the delta — which gaps were exploited and which remediations held. Where the work touches a payment-card, health-information, or other obligation, map only the applicable requirement after scope and evidence are checked, and distinguish an owner-confirmed exception from a control failure.

Evidence carries privacy and custody duties: preserve timestamped originals as the record and produce sanitized copies for the report; keep a chain-of-custody log for any captured physical item; redact faces and any third-party personal data before inclusion; and record audio only where lawful and confirmed in scope.

At close, reconcile every temporary artifact: retire the authorization record (letters do not roll over); rotate or re-issue any credential or badge that was captured or produced (with the owner); recover every dropped or implanted device and confirm its geo-fence/self-destruct and accountability state; reset anything altered under authority; and **verify owner-confirmed restoration rather than assuming cleanup because the window ended.** Hold a same-day debrief and a later formal after-action review as the engagement record.

Physical assessment is a hinge, so handoffs matter and each is a fresh authorization check, not an automatic grant. Physical entry to a jack hands into network access control (§20.10) and onward to the network method (§20.5); captured credentials or a compromised endpoint hand to the infrastructure method (§20.6); a reached server or network closet can hand to the database and network methods; networked physical-security and building-automation devices are network-tier targets once reachable. Preserve the authorized starting position and the tested endpoint across each handoff; a plausible cross-method chain is only as strong as its individually demonstrated links.

Automation is, for this discipline, a planning and post-engagement helper — public-source intelligence aggregation, evidence organization, and device-currency-to-advisory correlation — not an execution agent. Every entry decision, pretext, real-time navigation, confronted-situation response, chain-progression choice, and rules-of-engagement judgment is human-led. Any automation inherits the same facility and target allowlist, authorization bar, safety and disruption ceiling, custody rules, stop route, provenance, and human finding disposition. A future automation lane needs its own design, evaluation, and authority; this method does not imply that one has been deployed.

The output of this method is a reviewable account of what a person could and could not do at a defined facility, under defined authority, within a defined window — which controls prevented, detected, delayed, or responded, and which did not, each to the exact extent evidenced. It is neither a certification that a facility is secure nor a how-to for defeating one.
