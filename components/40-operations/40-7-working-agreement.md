# 40.7 · Penetration-Testing Team Working Agreement

> Framework component for adaptation. Publication does not authorize testing, certify conformance, or establish that an organization has adopted this practice.
> This is a team-owned agreement template, not employment policy, a leadership order, or proof that any organization uses these practices.

## What an agreement can do

A methodology tells testers what to examine. A working agreement tells a team how it will challenge an interpretation, ask for help, keep work visible, and protect the customer relationship while doing that work. A team should discuss and adopt its own version; copying a cadence or channel from another organization is not agreement.

Use this beside the practice's technical review, engagement, consulting, and evidence controls. Formal policies, employment rules, vendor terms, and emergency procedures take precedence. Contractors and external partners need explicit applicability decisions rather than accidental inclusion.

## The team makes these choices together

For each row, record a concrete local behavior, the system or forum where it happens, and how the team revisits it. A blank row is a decision to make, not a default rule.

| Topic | Question to resolve | Locally agreed behavior / owner / review trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Respectful challenge | How can a tester question an assumption or report interpretation without turning disagreement into a side conversation? | [Team decision] |
| Asking for help | What is the peer → specialist/lead → leadership route for a technical, scope, safety, customer, or policy concern? When may someone skip straight to the stop/contact authority? | [Team decision] |
| Blockers | How soon must a blocked engagement, evidence review, or customer response be made visible, and where is it recorded? | [Team decision] |
| Work state | Which system shows assignment, current status, decisions, owner, and next action? What counts as a meaningful update rather than a status ritual? | [Team decision] |
| Knowledge | Which lessons, tools, and decisions should become shared notes or method changes? Who checks that sensitive evidence and credentials stay out of broad channels? | [Team decision] |
| Meetings | Which recurring sessions earn their time, need an agenda, allow a pause or exit, and produce a decision or action? | [Team decision] |
| Focus and absence | How will the team protect testing/research time and cover work during leave, handoffs, and urgent demand? | [Team decision] |
| Customer communication | What does a useful scope explanation, difficult finding discussion, retest response, or correction of team error sound like? | [Team decision] |
| Learning and change | How can any member propose a change, try it, record disagreement, and revisit the agreement? | [Team decision] |

## A workable set of commitments

Teams may adopt, edit, or reject these examples:

1. **Assume there is context you do not yet have.** Ask a direct question and challenge the evidence or reasoning, not the person. It is acceptable to say “I do not know” or to revise a conclusion.
2. **Make blockers and safety concerns visible early.** Technical uncertainty goes to a peer or qualified reviewer; unresolved scope, authorization, policy, or customer impact goes to the named decision authority. Any tester can use the engagement stop path when conditions require it.
3. **Keep the work record alive.** A work item should show its owner, authorized scope, actual state, material decisions, dependencies, and handoff. Do not advance it merely because a meeting ended or a status field is due.
4. **Prepare for and improve meetings.** Use the shortest forum that can make the decision. Share context in advance when useful, distinguish required from optional participants, and leave with an owner and next action. Protect blocks for deep technical work and learning.
5. **Serve the person receiving the work.** Explain the finding, its evidence and limits, likely impact, and next practical step in terms the owner can use. Invite correction, own mistakes, and escalate collaboratively when the issue moves beyond the engagement.
6. **Share load and knowledge.** Pair on hard cases, review each other's technical claims, cover planned absences, and turn recurring confusion into a better method or answer rather than a permanent dependency on one person.

These commitments are intentionally not a universal one-business-day response promise, mandatory camera policy, meeting schedule, work-tracker product choice, or fixed hierarchy. Those are local conditions requiring team and organizational agreement.

## Use the agreement in a real situation

**Synthetic example:** a tester sees a possible high-impact authorization issue but cannot distinguish a server defect from an intentionally shared object. A customer asks for an immediate severity. The team record should show the observed request/response, the expected policy question, who will validate ownership, the scope and safety limit, and who may communicate an interim statement. A peer can challenge the interpretation; the engagement authority handles a scope expansion; a qualified reviewer and customer owner resolve the finding language. The agreement should make this path easy without pretending uncertainty has disappeared.

## Adopt, test, and revise

At a team working session, choose the local rows, explicitly mark any rejected examples, and identify the governing policy or procedure for emergencies and employment matters. Put the adopted agreement where the team can find it; give new members a chance to question it. At a retrospective or after a material failure, ask: Did the route work? Did someone depend on an undocumented side channel? Did a rule create busywork or a safety gap? Record the change and the next review trigger. The agreement earns its place only when the team actually uses it.
